<div class="text-block" margin-bottom:2em">
	I am a computational astrophysicist (PhD, <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/8hmfVkHoMkVkgKZX6" target="_blank">Universidad Autónoma de Madrid</a>, Spain, December 2019) specialising in galaxy formation and evolution, with a strong focus on methodological innovation, open and data-driven science, and the development of reproducible tools for the astronomical community.<br><br>	
	My scientific career spans multiple countries and research environments. Originally from the small Austrian town of <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/WUfLmS9GyYSc3DJU9" target="_blank">Weiz</a>, my early background was in business administration, information technology, and software development. I later made a decisive shift to physics and astrophysics at the <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/xAU7L8iSto1qfhLL6" target="_blank">Karl-Franzens University of Graz</a>, where I graduated in 2013.<br><br>
	I moved to Spain for my PhD and spent five years in Madrid (2015–2020). I then held my first postdoctoral position at the <a href="https://maps.app.goo.gl/i9oKYEAY7TXTE5L46" target="_blank">University of California, Santa Cruz</a> (2020–2021), before being awarded a <i>Margarita Salas Fellowship</i> in 2021 for the project <i>"Hidden Figures in the Sky</i>, which brought me to the <a href="https://goo.gl/maps/PoTjkoGYTP98atip6" target="_blank">Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile</a> in Santiago for two years (2022–2024).<br><br>
	Since 2024, I have been a member of the Computational Astrophysics group at the <a href="https://goo.gl/maps/iXK55VpNfonNfwgY8" target="_blank">Universidad de Sevilla</a> (Spain). I am involved in international collaborations across Europe, the Americas, and China. Since 2025, I have been an external collaborator at the <a href="https://www.universidadviu.com/es/" target="_blank">International University of Valencia</a>.<br><br>	
	My research combines cosmological simulations, machine learning, and data-intensive analyses to investigate how galaxies form and evolve across the full mass and surface-brightness spectrum. In particular, I focus on what I refer to as <i>“The Extremes”</i> of galaxy formation, ranging from diffuse <b>Low-Surface Brightness Galaxies</b> to <b>Bright Cluster Galaxies</b>. Although these systems occupy opposite ends of the galaxy population, they arise from the same underlying physical processes -- produced by the same Universe -- making them powerful laboratories for understanding galaxy assembly and the formation of large-scale structure in the cosmos.<br><br>	
	I am strongly committed to <b>cultural equity and inclusion in science</b>. I am a member of <a href="https://astronomersforplanet.earth/" target="_blank"><i>Astronomers for Planet Earth (A4E)</i></a> and the <a href="https://www.sea-astronomia.es/" target="_blank"><i>Sociedad Española de Astronomía (SEA)</i></a>. Teaching is a central part of my work, and I enjoy engaging students by making complex topics accessible through creative and innovative approaches. Alongside my research, I actively participate in outreach activities, regularly giving talks in schools and serving as a <i>“women in science”</i> representative to promote inclusion and visibility.<br><br>	
	In recognition of my scientific work and community engagement, I was named <a href="https://oepg.at/de/news/2025-05-01-physikerin-des-monats-doris-stoppacher" target="_blank"><i>Physicist of the Month</i></a> by the Austrian Physical Society in 2025. Outside academia, I am actively involved in comedy presentation and improvisational theatre, which strongly informs my communication style and creative approach to science.
</div>
